## The short answer
Mice enter garages through tiny openings, food scraps, and clutter; to keep them out, seal every gap, remove attractants, store items in rodent‑proof containers, and add non‑toxic deterrents such as RepelNest repellent pouches. Follow these steps and you will maintain a mouse‑free garage year‑round.

## Identify entry points
Mice can squeeze through holes the size of a dime. Walk the perimeter of your garage and inspect walls, foundation, doors, and windows. If the garage shares a wall with the house, continue that inspection along the foundation and into any [basement](/outdoor/how-to-keep-mice-out-of-basement/) on the same slab. Look for gnaw marks, shredded insulation, and small holes around utility lines. Pay special attention to the space under the garage door, as the weather‑strip can wear thin and create a gap. Use a flashlight at night to spot fresh droppings that often indicate hidden pathways.

## Seal gaps and entry points
**Inspect and repair**: Use steel‑wool or copper mesh to fill holes larger than 1/4 inch, then cover with expanding spray foam or cement. For gaps around pipes, install metal flashing. Replace damaged door sweeps and weather‑stripping.

### Apply durable materials
- **Steel wool**: Mice cannot chew through it; pack tightly into holes before sealing.
- **Hardware cloth**: Secure with screws to cover larger openings like vent openings.
- **Spray foam**: Expands to fill irregular cracks; choose a product that adheres to concrete and wood.

Seal every opening before the next season. A completely closed perimeter eliminates the primary invitation for rodents. For extra peace of mind, consider installing a metal door sweep that overlaps the threshold by at least half an inch.

## Reduce attractants
Mice are opportunistic feeders. Remove any food source that could lure them into the garage.

- **Store feed and pet food** in airtight plastic bins. Glass jars are also effective.
- **Clean up spills** immediately. Use a broom and a damp mop to remove crumbs from the floor.
- **Dispose of cardboard and paper** in sealed bags; mice love nesting in these materials.
- **Trim vegetation** near the garage walls. Overhanging branches provide a bridge for rodents.

## Organize storage strategically
Clutter creates hiding places. Install shelving that keeps items off the concrete floor and away from walls. Use clear plastic bins with tight‑fitting lids rather than cardboard boxes. Label each bin so you can locate items quickly and avoid unnecessary rummaging that disturbs hidden nests. The article on [how to store garden tools so they don’t rust](/tools/how-to-store-garden-tools-so-they-dont-rust/) offers additional tips for keeping metal items dry and organized, which also reduces moisture that attracts insects and, indirectly, mice.

## Use traps and deterrents wisely
Traditional snap traps and live‑catch traps can reduce an existing population, but they work best when combined with exclusion methods.

- **Snap traps**: Place along walls, behind stored items, and near suspected runways. Bait with peanut butter for a strong scent.
- **Live traps**: Check them daily. If you use this option, follow your local rules on whether relocation is allowed.
- **Ultrasonic devices**: Emit high‑frequency sounds; effectiveness varies, so treat them as supplemental.

### Natural deterrents
- **Peppermint oil**: Soak cotton balls and place near entry points. Refresh weekly.
- **Steel‑wool plugs**: Insert into small holes; mice cannot chew through.

## Additional maintenance tips
- **Seasonal check**: In early summer, re‑inspect for new gaps caused by expansion.
- **Ventilation**: Keep the garage well‑ventilated to reduce humidity, which can attract insects that in turn draw mice.
- **Lighting**: Bright lights deter nocturnal rodents; consider motion‑sensor LEDs for occasional use.
- **Pet safety**: If you have dogs or cats that roam the garage, ensure any traps are placed out of reach.

## Seasonal considerations
Weather changes can open new pathways. In winter, concrete contracts and may develop hairline cracks; a quick spray‑foam touch‑up before the first freeze can prevent mice from exploiting them. In spring, melting snow can raise the ground level, exposing previously buried pipe penetrations. Use this season to re‑apply steel‑wool plugs to any low‑lying openings you may have missed. Summer brings higher humidity, which can soften wood and cause warping around doors—inspect door frames and replace any warped weather‑stripping promptly. Fall is the ideal time for a comprehensive audit: walk the entire perimeter, test door sweeps with a piece of cardboard, and replace any worn‑out hardware cloth.

## Using technology to stay ahead
Modern homeowners can augment traditional methods with smart tools. Battery‑operated motion sensors that trigger LED lights can be mounted near suspected runways; the sudden illumination startles mice and discourages repeated travel. Wireless rodent monitoring kits, available from several retailers, send alerts to your phone when activity is detected, allowing you to intervene before an infestation takes hold. For a low‑tech option, place a small dish of powdered cayenne pepper in corners; the scent is unpleasant to rodents and the powder can be refreshed monthly. Pair these tech‑enhanced tactics with your existing RepelNest pouches for a layered defense that adapts to seasonal shifts.

## Monitoring and follow‑up
After implementing the steps, set a weekly schedule to look for fresh droppings, gnaw marks, or new holes. Use a flashlight to examine dark corners. If signs reappear, revisit the sealing checklist and consider adding another set of RepelNest pouches for reinforced deterrence. Document any new activity in a simple log; noting the location and date helps you spot patterns and target problem areas more efficiently.

## Summary
Keeping mice out of your garage is a matter of exclusion, sanitation, and consistent deterrence. Seal every opening, eliminate food sources, organize storage, and use safe repellents like RepelNest pouches.
