## The short answer

You can clean patio pavers thoroughly with a standard garden hose by relying on water volume and mechanical agitation rather than extreme pressure. Clear all furniture, sweep away loose debris, wet the stone, and scrub it in sections using warm water mixed with mild dish soap or oxygen bleach and a stiff-bristled broom. Rinse the surface with a wide spray pattern directed across the joints rather than directly down into them. 

High-pressure machines often carve into soft concrete surfaces, erode protective sealers, and blow out the polymeric sand binding the pavers. A garden hose avoids these problems. While it requires more physical scrubbing to lift embedded stains, it protects the structural foundation of your patio.

## Why avoid high pressure on pavers?

Pressure washers look efficient in short video clips. In practice, high-pressure equipment frequently damages residential hardscaping. Concrete pavers, clay bricks, and natural flagstones carry porous surfaces that high pressure can pit or etch.

According to technical guidance from the [Interlocking Concrete Pavement Institute](https://icpi.org/), improper washing techniques frequently wash out joint sand, destabilizing the interlocking pavement system. Once joint sand disappears, pavers shift under foot traffic, edges chip, and weeds sprout in the damp voids.

Additionally, high-velocity water can strip the factory seal or topical sealant applied to concrete pavers. A standard garden hose running at municipal pressure (typically 40 to 60 pounds per square inch) supplies enough water flow to float away lifted dirt without compromising your patio's joints.

## Tools and materials you need

Gather your supplies before wetting the stone. You do not need expensive specialty chemicals for routine maintenance.

* **Garden hose:** A standard 5/8-inch or 3/4-inch hose that provides steady water flow.
* **Adjustable hose nozzle:** A nozzle with a fan, flat, or shower setting to control the stream.
* **Stiff-bristled push broom or deck brush:** Synthetic bristles work well on concrete and stone. Avoid wire-bristle brushes, which scratch pavers and leave behind steel flecks that rust.
* **Cleaning solution:** Mild dish soap (such as blue Dawn) or oxygenated bleach powder (sodium percarbonate). Avoid chlorine bleach near lawn edges, as noted by the [Penn State Extension](https://extension.psu.edu/), because runoff damages turfgrass, landscape shrubs, and soil biology.
* **Five-gallon bucket:** For mixing warm soapy water.
* **Replacement polymeric sand:** Keep a small bag on hand to top off joints if any loose sand washes out.

## Step-by-step cleaning process

Follow these steps on an overcast day or during the cooler hours of early morning. Direct sun dries soapy water too quickly, leaving mineral haze and soap scum on paver surfaces.

### 1. Clear and sweep the surface
Move all patio tables, chairs, planters, and fire pits off the paved area. Use a dry push broom to clear away surface leaves, twigs, dirt, and loose gravel. If you leave dry leaves on the stone when you turn on the water, they can bleed organic tannins into the pavers, leaving brown rings.

### 2. Protect adjacent plants
Before applying any cleaning mix, soak nearby garden beds, grass edges, and shrub roots with clean water from your garden hose. Saturated soil does not readily absorb soap solutions. You can also evaluate simpler spray options like [cleaning tablets to replace spray bottles](/cleaning/do-cleaning-tablets-replace-spray-bottles/) for wiping down outdoor dining tables before moving them back.

### 3. Pre-wet the pavers
Spray the entire patio with a gentle fan setting from your hose. Wetting the pavers prevents the stone from immediately absorbing your soap solution into its core. The goal is a damp surface, not standing puddles.

### 4. Mix and apply the cleaning solution
In your five-gallon bucket, mix warm water with 1/4 cup of mild dish soap. For persistent dark patches or biological film, mix oxygen bleach powder with warm water according to package instructions. Pour the mixture evenly across a 10-foot by 10-foot section. Working in manageable sections prevents the soap from drying before you can scrub.

### 5. Scrub with a stiff broom
Use your deck brush or stiff push broom to work the solution into the surface texture. Scrub along paver edges and crosswise across joints. Do not dig bristles directly parallel into the joint channels, which dislodges sand. Let the soapy foam sit on the stone for 5 to 10 minutes to loosen atmospheric grime, barbecue grease, and pollen.

### 6. Rinse thoroughly with the garden hose
Set your hose nozzle to a flat or sweep pattern. Hold the spray tip roughly 12 to 18 inches above the surface. Rinse the loosened dirt toward your patio's drainage edge. Angle the spray across the pavers rather than straight down into joint lines to keep the sand undisturbed. Repeat across all sections until runoff water runs clear.

### 7. Inspect joints and let dry
Let the patio dry completely over 24 to 48 hours. Once dry, examine the joints. If you notice recessed spots or exposed paver edges, sweep dry polymeric sand into the gaps, tamp it gently, sweep off all surface dust, and mist it according to the sand manufacturer's instructions to lock it in place.

## Handling specific stains

Different marks require specific approaches. Household items often handle them without high water pressure.

* **Grease and oil:** Fresh oil from grills should be blotted immediately with absorbent cat litter, cornstarch, or baking soda. Let the powder sit for an hour to pull oil from the stone. Sweep it up, apply liquid dish soap directly to the spot, scrub with a handheld nylon brush, and rinse with your hose.
* **Moss and algae:** Light green or black biological film lifts with warm water and oxygen bleach. If green growth appears frequently in shaded zones, trimming overhanging branches improves sunlight and airflow, keeping the stone drier. 
* **Leaf and mulch stains:** Organic tannins fade over time with sun exposure, but a scrub with oxygen bleach and warm water accelerates the process without etching concrete.
* **Rust spots:** Outdoor furniture legs often shed rust rings. Avoid muriatic acid, which severely etches concrete. Instead, use an oxalic acid-based deck or masonry cleaner diluted according to label directions, scrub lightly, and rinse with copious water.

## Pros and cons

Choosing a garden hose over mechanical pressure washing involves clear trade-offs between physical effort and surface safety.

### Pros
* **Protects joint integrity:** Low to moderate water pressure keeps joint sand and polymeric sealants intact.
* **Prevents surface etching:** Stone and concrete paver faces will not pit, scale, or streak from harsh spray tips.
* **Low equipment cost:** Requires no rented machinery, gas motors, extension cords, or complex maintenance.
* **Safer for surrounding landscapes:** Avoids high-pressure overspray that strips bark, shreds flowers, and splashes dirty water onto home siding.

### Cons
* **Requires manual labor:** You must physically scrub the stone with a broom to dislodge embedded grime.
* **Slower on large areas:** Cleaning a broad patio section by section takes more time than using a mechanical surface cleaner.
* **Limited reach on deep stains:** Deeply embedded motor oil or years of neglected moss may require repeated scrub sessions.

## Who it is for, and who should skip it

Cleaning pavers with a garden hose is ideal for homeowners who want safe, routine spring and autumn maintenance on residential patios, walkways, and pool decks. It suits anyone looking to preserve expensive polymeric joint sand, decorative paver sealers, and nearby garden beds without renting machinery. If your patio was installed recently or shows signs of crumbly joints, low-pressure hose washing is the safest option available.

Skip this method if you manage an extensive commercial plaza or an neglected patio covered in thick, hardened layers of dried mortar, calcified efflorescence, or industrial grease. Those situations require professional hot-water remediation, chemical poultices, or carefully controlled rotary surface cleaners operated by a certified hardscape contractor.

## Maintaining your patio after washing

Once your patio is clean and dry, minimal upkeep reduces the need for heavy scrubbing in future seasons.

Sweep off dry organic matter weekly. Decaying leaves and lawn clippings trap moisture and deposit tannins that discolour pavers. Keep gutters clear so rainwater does not overflow and cascade across specific paver sections, which leads to localized algae blooms.

If you regularly use your outdoor space for summer cooking, check your preparation setup. Just as learning whether [titanium cutting boards dull knives](/kitchen/do-titanium-cutting-boards-dull-knives/) helps you organize indoor meal prep, keeping a dedicated grill mat beneath outdoor barbecues protects paver stones from hot fat splatters before they soak into concrete.
