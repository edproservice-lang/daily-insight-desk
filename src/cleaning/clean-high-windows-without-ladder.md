## The short answer

To clean high indoor windows without a ladder, attach a microfiber washer and a squeegee to an extension pole, wet the glass with a mild cleaning solution, and squeegee from the top down in overlapping strokes. For large, smooth panes you clean often, a suction-mounted window-cleaning robot does the reaching for you while you stay on the floor.

## Check the window before you start

A few minutes of planning saves a lot of reaching. Stand back and look at the glass you need to clean:

- **Height and width.** Measure roughly from the floor to the top of the pane so you can pick a pole that reaches with your arms in a comfortable position, not fully stretched overhead.
- **Glass type.** Large, smooth panes are the easiest case for both pole tools and robots. Divided-light windows with muntins need a smaller washer pad to get into the corners.
- **What is between you and the glass.** Move furniture, plants, and lamps out of the way so you can stand close and look straight up.
- **Condition.** Note any cracked glass or loose glazing and leave those panes alone until they are repaired.

## Tools for cleaning high windows without a ladder

You do not need a closet of gear. A short, well-chosen kit handles almost every interior window:

- **Extension pole** with a threaded tip, long enough to reach the top of your highest pane.
- **Microfiber strip washer** (also called a sleeve or scrubber) to wet and loosen dust.
- **Squeegee** that fits the same pole, ideally with a swivel head so you can keep the blade flat to the glass from below.
- **Microfiber pad or flat mop head** for drying edges and frames.
- **Bucket or spray bottle** with a mild cleaning solution.
- **Old towels** to protect the sill and floor from drips.

For the solution, plain warm water with a few drops of dish soap is enough for most dusty indoor glass. If you prefer a mix you can keep on hand, our guide to [making a reusable all-purpose cleaner](/cleaning/make-reusable-all-purpose-cleaner/) includes a glass-friendly variation. Another simple route is one refillable bottle and concentrate; this overview of whether [cleaning tablets can replace spray bottles](/cleaning/do-cleaning-tablets-replace-spray-bottles/) explains how that setup works, and if you are trimming the clutter under the sink, see [how to stop buying cleaning sprays](/cleaning/how-to-stop-buying-cleaning-sprays/).

## How to clean high indoor windows step by step

### Step 1: Dust the frame and sill first

Run a dry microfiber pad on the pole along the top of the frame and the sill. Dry dust turns into muddy streaks the moment it meets your cleaning solution, so removing it first keeps the glass cleaner and your washer pad lasting longer.

### Step 2: Wet the glass with the washer

Dip the strip washer in the solution, squeeze out most of the liquid, and attach it to the pole. Work from the top of the pane down, scrubbing lightly side to side. A damp washer is better than a dripping one indoors; it loosens dust and fingerprints without soaking the sill.

Kitchen windows often carry a light film of cooking grease. Give those panes a second pass with the washer, the same way you would treat [greasy kitchen counters](/cleaning/how-to-clean-greasy-kitchen-counters/), before you squeegee.

### Step 3: Squeegee from the top down

Swap the washer for the squeegee. Start at the top corner, press the blade flat against the glass, and pull straight down in one steady stroke. Wipe the blade on a towel, then start the next stroke so it overlaps the last one slightly. Repeat across the pane. A swivel head makes it much easier to keep even pressure when the window is well above your head.

### Step 4: Detail the edges

Water tends to collect along the frame and bottom edge. Put a dry microfiber pad on the pole and press it along the edges and corners to lift the last line of water. Wipe the sill with a towel as you finish each window.

## Getting a streak-free finish from the floor

Most streaks come from three things: too much soap, a dirty blade, or glass that dries before you wipe it. Keep these habits and high windows look clear from any angle:

- **Use less soap than you think.** A few drops per bucket is plenty for indoor glass.
- **Keep the blade clean.** Wipe it after every stroke, and replace a nicked rubber blade.
- **Mind the sun.** Clean windows that are in shade or on an overcast day so the solution does not dry mid-stroke.
- **Check from a different angle.** Step to the side and look at the glass against the light; missed spots show up immediately and are easy to touch up with the dry pad.

## Stay safe while you work

Keeping your feet on the floor is the biggest safety win. The U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission notes that [falls from ladders can cause injuries ranging from bruises to fractures](https://www.cpsc.gov/s3fs-public/Home_Safe_Outdoor-Repairs_checklist_COVID19_FINAL.pdf), and that older adults face a higher risk, so a pole or robot is a smart choice for anyone who would rather not climb.

A few floor-level habits still matter. Wear shoes with good grip, keep the bucket and cords out of your path, and dry drips on hard floors right away. Look up before you extend a long pole so you do not hit a ceiling fan or light fixture. If you use a store-bought cleaner, follow the label and open a window for fresh air; the EPA explains that many household cleaning products [release volatile organic compounds while you use them](https://www.epa.gov/indoor-air-quality-iaq/volatile-organic-compounds-impact-indoor-air-quality) and recommends good ventilation.
